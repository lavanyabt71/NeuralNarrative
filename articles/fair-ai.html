Can we make AI fair?

An algorithm can be consistent and still produce unfair outcomes. Fairness requires asking what data, goals, and assumptions shaped the system.

WHO
DECIDES?

Bias can enter before the model

Training data reflects the world it came from, including gaps and historical inequalities. If some groups are underrepresented or past decisions were unfair, a model may learn patterns that repeat those problems.

There isn't one fairness metric

Different definitions of fairness can conflict. Equal error rates, equal opportunity, and similar selection rates measure different things. Choosing among them requires context, stakeholder input, and a clear understanding of who may be harmed.

Build accountability into the process

Useful safeguards include representative data, evaluation across relevant groups, documentation, human review for high-impact decisions, and a way for affected people to challenge outcomes. Monitoring must continue after launch because real-world conditions change.

THE KEY IDEA

Fair AI is not a checkbox. It is an ongoing practice of measuring impact, explaining decisions, and taking responsibility.
